Free up Gmail storage — without paying for Google One

Your Google account gives you 15 GB shared across Gmail, Drive and Photos. Newsletters you never open quietly eat it. Here's how to win the space back — for free.

Step 1: Stop the inflow (the part everyone forgets)

Deleting old mail is treating the symptom. The junk keeps coming back next week. The real fix is to stop the newsletters at the source — which is exactly what DontMailMe does. It runs a free script inside your own Gmail that auto-unsubscribes you from the lists you never read, so your storage stops filling up in the first place.

Set up auto-unsubscribe — free

Step 2: Clear what's already there

To reclaim space you've already lost, delete the bulk of old, heavy mail. In the Gmail search box, try these one at a time, select all, and delete:

  • category:promotions older_than:1y — a year of promotional clutter
  • larger:10M — the big attachments quietly hogging gigabytes
  • from:(noreply) older_than:6m — automated mail you'll never re-read

Then empty your Trash and Spam folders. This is the step most people miss: deleted mail sits in Trash for 30 days and still counts against your quota. Only permanently deleted mail actually frees space (per Google).

An honest note

Unsubscribing prevents future storage growth; the bulk-delete above reclaims what you've already used. Do both once and you'll likely never see the "storage full" warning again — and you won't be paying Google a monthly fee to store mail you never wanted. See the full impact and methodology.

Will unsubscribing free up my Google storage?
Unsubscribing stops new newsletters from filling your shared 15 GB Google limit, so it prevents future storage growth. To reclaim space you've already used, you also need to delete old mail and empty your Trash and Spam — only permanently deleted mail frees storage, per Google.
How do I free up Gmail storage for free?
First stop the junk arriving by auto-unsubscribing from newsletters. Then, in Gmail, search category:promotions older_than:1y or larger:10M, delete those messages, and empty your Trash and Spam folders. This reclaims space without paying for Google One.
