Gmail "Manage Subscriptions" vs. automatic unsubscribing

In July 2025 Gmail added a "Manage subscriptions" view. It's a welcome step — and for some people it's enough. Here's the honest difference.

What Gmail's "Manage subscriptions" does

It shows your most frequent senders and lets you unsubscribe from them with a click, sending the request on your behalf. It's built in, it's free, and for an occasional tidy-up it works well. If you just want to clear a handful of senders inside Gmail once in a while, use it.

Where automatic unsubscribing goes further

  • Set-and-forget vs. manual. Gmail's tool is something you remember to open and work through, one sender at a time. DontMailMe runs on a schedule and keeps your inbox clean without you thinking about it.
  • Beyond Gmail. Manage subscriptions is Gmail-only. DontMailMe also covers Outlook and Apple Mail.
  • Zero-data by design. With Gmail's feature, Google handles everything. DontMailMe's script runs entirely inside your own account — no company, including us, sees your inbox. And it's open source, so you can verify that.
  • Same safe standard. Both use the official one-click unsubscribe; DontMailMe is built around RFC 8058 and never touches links in the email body.

Which should you use?

Use Gmail's built-in tool for a quick, occasional cleanup inside Gmail. Use DontMailMe when you want it to keep happening automatically, across more than just Gmail, with nothing leaving your own cloud. They're not mutually exclusive — but only one of them is set-and-forget.

Is Gmail's built-in "Manage subscriptions" enough?
For occasional cleanup, yes. Gmail's Manage subscriptions view (launched July 2025) lets you unsubscribe one sender at a time. But it is manual, Gmail-only, and Google processes everything. If you want set-and-forget unsubscribing that also covers Outlook and Apple Mail and keeps your data out of everyone's hands, an automatic tool like DontMailMe goes further.
How is DontMailMe different from Gmail's Manage subscriptions?
Gmail's tool is manual and one sender at a time, inside Gmail only. DontMailMe runs automatically on a schedule, works across Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail, is open source, and runs entirely in your own account so no company — including us — sees your inbox.
