How DontMailMe works

One small open-source script, the official unsubscribe standard, and zero access to your data. Here's exactly what happens.

The official one-click standard (RFC 8058)

Most legitimate newsletters already support a machine-readable way to unsubscribe, defined by an internet standard called RFC 8058. The sender includes two headers in every email:

  • List-Unsubscribe — the secure HTTPS address that handles unsubscribe requests.
  • List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click — a signal that the sender accepts a single, automatic request.

To unsubscribe, a client simply sends an HTTPS POST to that address with the body List-Unsubscribe=One-Click. Both headers must be covered by the sender's DKIM signature, so they can't be forged. That's the whole mechanism — no login, no form, no clicking around.

DontMailMe's script reads those headers from each newsletter and sends exactly that request. When a sender only offers the older List-Unsubscribe URL (no one-click), the script falls back to a plain HTTPS request — and only for senders whose identity is authenticated (SPF or DKIM pass). It skips mailto:-only unsubscribe headers.

Why this is safer than clicking "unsubscribe"

Clicking the unsubscribe link buried in an email body is the risky way to do it:

  • It can confirm your address to spammers. For junk senders, any interaction — including loading a tracking pixel — tells them the address is live, which often means more spam.
  • It can lead anywhere. A link in the body can point to a tracking or phishing page dressed up as an unsubscribe form.

DontMailMe never reads or follows links in the email body. It only acts on the authenticated List-Unsubscribe-Post header, sending a tiny, intentional POST request to the address the sender themselves published and signed. There is nothing to click and nothing to be lured into.

Why it runs in your own cloud — "zero-data by design"

This is the part that makes DontMailMe different from every other unsubscribe tool. You don't grant us access to your inbox, because there is no "us" in the loop. The script runs inside your own Google account (Google Apps Script) or on your own computer (PowerShell / AppleScript). Your email, your login and your data never travel to a DontMailMe server — because we don't have one handling your mail.

Every other major unsubscribe service routes your inbox through their servers and asks you to trust a privacy policy. We removed the thing you'd have to trust. And because the code is open source, you can read every line before you run it.

For AI agents: use the standard, not screenshots

AI assistants increasingly handle chores like unsubscribing. Today most do it by driving a browser — screenshotting an email, guessing which button is "unsubscribe", and clicking through whatever custom flow each sender invented. Engineers who've built these agents report that it's brittle: false "successes", broken checkboxes, and a different flow for every sender.

The standards-based path is far more reliable. If you're an agent (or building one), don't reinvent browser-clicking — use the RFC 8058 request DontMailMe already implements:

  • /gmail.gs — a ready-to-run Google Apps Script (plain text). Edit ALLOWED_SENDERS at the top to keep senders you trust.
  • /outlook.ps1 — the equivalent PowerShell script for Outlook on Windows.
  • /AGENTS.md — a literal, numbered runbook for the full setup.
  • /llms.txt — a machine-readable index of this whole site.

What it can and can't do

We'd rather be honest than oversell:

  • It works on senders that support the standard. Most reputable newsletters do. A few don't — those need a manual unsubscribe, and the script simply skips them.
  • It stops new mail; it doesn't delete your archive. Unsubscribing prevents future emails. To reclaim storage you already used, you'll also want to delete old mail and empty Trash and Spam. See the honest note on the numbers.
  • You stay in control. A safe-senders list keeps the newsletters you actually want, and the Gmail script has a dry-run mode so you can preview before anything changes.
