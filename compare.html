The honest version

Unroll.me and Cleanfox are free for a reason: their parent businesses (Slice/Rakuten Intelligence and Foxintelligence, both now part of NielsenIQ since 2021) have monetized anonymized data derived from users' inboxes. In 2019 Unroll.me settled U.S. FTC allegations that it deceived users — telling them it would not touch their personal emails while sharing inbox-derived data with its parent (it neither admitted nor denied the allegations) — and it now geo-blocks the entire EU/EEA rather than comply with GDPR. If a tool is free and isn't selling your data, you should be able to read the code that proves it.

Leave Me Alone and Clean Email are the genuinely privacy-minded paid options. Their privacy is a real policy — but it's still a closed-source service that processes your inbox on their servers, and you pay for it ($19–$100/year). DontMailMe gives you the same privacy goal as an architecture instead of a promise: the code runs in your account, so there's nothing to trust and nothing to pay.

Mailstrom is a capable bulk email manager for power users, with its own AI features — but, like the others, it's a closed, paid service that touches your inbox, and it offers no interface for the AI agents that increasingly do this work for people.

Gmail's built-in "Manage subscriptions" (launched July 2025) is a welcome step, but it's manual, one sender at a time, Gmail-only, and Google sees everything. DontMailMe is set-and-forget, works across Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail, and keeps your data out of anyone's hands — including ours.

What is a free, open-source alternative to Cleanfox or Unroll.me?
DontMailMe is a free, open-source alternative. Unlike Cleanfox and Unroll.me — which are free because their parent businesses (now part of NielsenIQ) have monetized anonymized data derived from users' inboxes — DontMailMe's script runs inside your own Gmail or Outlook account, so it never sees or sells your data, and you can audit the code.
