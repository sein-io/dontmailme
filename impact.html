The hidden cost of the newsletters you never read

Email feels weightless. At scale, it isn't. The good news: stopping the ones you never open is one of the easiest footprints to cut.

29.7M tCO₂e from spam email each year — more than some entire countries emit
84.8%of all email sent is spam (≈271 billion messages a day)
~40 kgCO₂e for one newsletter sent to 10,000 people who mostly never open it
~184 kgCO₂e from email for a typical office worker per year

Sources: Today Testing (spam footprint); Carbon Literacy Project and Mike Berners-Lee, How Bad Are Bananas? (per-email and per-worker figures). Figures are estimates — see the methodology and honesty note below.

How we calculate your impact

We keep the math simple and transparent. The calculator on the home page uses these assumptions:

  • ~2 emails per week, per newsletter — a conservative average for a promotional list (≈104 emails a year that you no longer receive for each unsubscribe).
  • ~4 g CO₂e per promotional email — the Carbon Literacy Project's widely-cited figure for a "standard" email (their range runs from 0.3 g for spam to 50 g for an email with a large attachment; these estimates vary and are debated).
  • ~0.16 kg CO₂e per kilometre driven — an average passenger car, used for the "driving avoided" comparison.
  • ~20 kg CO₂ absorbed per tree, per year — One Tree Planted's working figure for a maturing tree.
  • ~€5 donated plants one tree — half of each pay-what-you-want gift goes to One Tree Planted (~€2.50 per tree); the other half funds the project.

So unsubscribing from 20 newsletters means roughly 2,080 fewer emails a year and about 8 kg CO₂e not emitted — every year, automatically, because the mail simply stops being sent to you.

Why we plant trees

DontMailMe is free. It's funded by pay-what-you-want donations: half of every donation goes to One Tree Planted, the other half keeps the project alive and open source. In practice, roughly every €5 you give plants one tree (about €2.50 of it, at ~€2.50 per tree) — and a maturing tree absorbs about 20 kg of CO₂ a year, on top of the emissions your cleanup already prevents.

An honest note on the numbers

Carbon figures for email vary widely between studies, and we'd rather be straight with you than impressive:

  • Unsubscribing stops new mail. It doesn't, by itself, free storage you've already used — for that you also need to delete old mail and empty your Trash and Spam folders.
  • The saving is "emails never sent," not a one-time delete. Critics rightly note that the energy you spend deleting an email can exceed the email's own footprint. We count the future sends you prevent — a recurring saving — not the act of deletion.
  • Estimates are estimates. We use mid-range, sourced figures and show our working so you can judge for yourself.

The point isn't to guilt you into saving the planet one inbox at a time. It's that a cleaner, calmer inbox happens to be good for the planet too — so the good part comes along for free.
